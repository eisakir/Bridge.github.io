Learn Contract Bridge

A friendly guide for complete beginners. No previous card-game experience required.

Bridge is one of the world's most enjoyable card games. It combines teamwork, planning, communication, and careful play. Although it has a reputation for being difficult, anyone can learn the basics with a little guidance.

By the end of this guide you'll understand enough to sit down, play a social game, and continue learning with confidence.

The Auction

Before any cards are played, players participate in an auction (also called the bidding).

The purpose of the auction is to determine:

  • Which partnership will try to make the contract.
  • How many tricks they promise to win.
  • Whether a trump suit will be used.

Who Starts?

The dealer always makes the first call. Play then proceeds clockwise.

Dealer → Left-Hand Opponent → Partner → Right-Hand Opponent → Repeat

Legal Bids

Bids begin at 1♣ and continue upward through 7 No Trump.

Example Bid Meaning
1♣ Promise to win 7 tricks with Clubs as trump.
2♠ Promise to win 8 tricks with Spades as trump.
3NT Promise to win 9 tricks with no trump suit.
4♥ Promise to win 10 tricks with Hearts as trump.
6♦ Promise to win 12 tricks.
7NT Promise to win all 13 tricks.

Other Calls

Pass

Choose not to make a bid.

Double

Challenge the opponents' contract under specific conditions.

Redouble

Respond to a double, increasing the stakes again.

What Is a Contract?

The highest bid becomes the contract. For example:

4♥

Means the partnership promises to win 10 tricks with Hearts as trump.

Declarer and Dummy

The first player in the partnership who bid the final trump suit (or No Trump) becomes the declarer. The declarer's partner becomes the dummy.

Example 1

Auction

North: 1♥

East: Pass

South: 2♥

West: Pass

North: 4♥

Everyone Passes

North was the first player in the partnership to bid Hearts, so North becomes the declarer. South becomes the dummy.

Example 2

Auction

East: 1NT

South: Pass

West: 3NT

North: Pass

East: Pass

South: Pass

East bid No Trump first, so East becomes the declarer.

Playing the Hand

Once the auction has finished, the play begins.

Opening Lead

The player to the left of the declarer plays the first card. This is called the opening lead.

1

Opening Lead

The defender on declarer's left leads the first card.

2

Dummy Appears

The dummy places all 13 cards face up on the table.

3

Declarer Plays

The declarer chooses cards for both their own hand and the dummy.

4

Play Continues

Players continue until all 13 tricks have been played.

The Dummy

After the opening lead, the dummy spreads every card face up. Everyone can now see the dummy's cards. Only the declarer decides which card the dummy should play.

Following Suit

Whenever possible, you must play a card in the same suit as the opening lead.

Example

West leads the ♠10.

If you have any spades, you must play one. You cannot choose another suit unless you have no spades left.

Winning a Trick

Each trick contains one card from every player. Normally, the highest card in the suit that was led wins the trick.

Example Trick

Player Card
West ♣8
North ♣Q
East ♣K
South ♣5

East wins the trick because the King is the highest club played.

Trump Cards

If the contract names a trump suit, any trump card beats every card in another suit.

Example

Contract: 4♥

West ♠K
North ♠A
East ♠7
South ♥2

South wins the trick because Hearts are trumps.

No Trump Contracts

In a No Trump contract there is no trump suit. Every trick is won by the highest card of the suit led.

Working Together

Declarer

Tries to make the contract by winning enough tricks.

Dummy

Places cards on the table and follows the declarer's instructions.

Defenders

Cooperate to prevent the declarer from making the contract.

Scoring

The full Bridge scoring system is detailed, but beginners only need a few important ideas to start playing.

Contract Level

A contract always promises six tricks plus the bid level.

  • 1-level = 7 tricks
  • 2-level = 8 tricks
  • 3-level = 9 tricks
  • 4-level = 10 tricks
  • 5-level = 11 tricks
  • 6-level = 12 tricks
  • 7-level = 13 tricks

Overtricks

If you win more tricks than your contract requires, the extra tricks are called overtricks.

Undertricks

If you win fewer tricks than promised, the missing tricks are called undertricks.

Example

Contract: 4♠

Required tricks: 10

  • 10 tricks = Contract made
  • 11 tricks = One overtrick
  • 12 tricks = Two overtricks
  • 9 tricks = One undertrick

Tips for Your First Game

Relax

Every experienced Bridge player was once a beginner. Don't worry about making mistakes.

Count Your Cards

Before the auction begins, make sure you have exactly 13 cards.

Take Your Time

There is no need to rush. Think before making a bid or playing a card.

Watch Every Card

Pay attention to every trick. Even small cards can become important later.

Ask Questions Afterwards

During friendly games, most players are happy to explain hands after they finish.

Enjoy Yourself

Bridge is a lifelong game. Focus on learning rather than winning.

Common Beginner Mistakes

Forgetting to Follow Suit

If you have a card in the suit led, you must play one. This is one of the most important rules in Bridge.

Playing Too Quickly

Pause for a moment before each play. Consider what your opponents may have.

Ignoring the Dummy

The dummy's cards are a valuable resource. Look at both hands before planning the play.

Miscounting Tricks

Keep track of how many tricks your side has already won.

Overbidding

Promising more tricks than your cards can realistically win often leads to poor results.

Giving Up

Even difficult contracts can sometimes succeed with careful planning.

Bridge Etiquette

✓ Be polite to everyone at the table.
✓ Let each player think without rushing them.
✓ Avoid commenting on hands while they are still being played.
✓ Congratulate good play by either partnership.
✓ Learn from mistakes instead of blaming your partner.
✓ Remember that Bridge is meant to be enjoyable.

Glossary

The bidding phase before the cards are played.

Bid

A promise to win a certain number of tricks.

Contract

The final winning bid of the auction.

Declarer

The player who plays both their own hand and the dummy.

Double

A call challenging the opponents' contract.

A suit containing exactly two cards.

A successful contract worth less than game.

A response to a double.

Revoke

Failing to follow suit when able.

Trump

A suit that beats every non-trump suit.

Void

A suit in which a player has no cards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Bridge difficult to learn?

Bridge has a lot of depth, but the basic rules are much easier than many people expect. Once you understand tricks, bidding, and partnerships, you can already enjoy casual games.

Do I need to memorize every rule?

No. Learn the fundamentals first. Your understanding will naturally improve with experience.

How many people are required?

Contract Bridge is designed for exactly four players, arranged as two partnerships.

Can beginners play together?

Absolutely. Many people learn fastest by playing friendly practice games where everyone is learning together.

What should I learn next?

Once you're comfortable with the mechanics, start learning a beginner bidding system such as Standard American or Acol, then practice declarer play and defense.
