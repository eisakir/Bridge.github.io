Welcome!

Bridge is a partnership trick-taking card game played by four people. Unlike many card games, Bridge includes an auction before anyone plays a card. During the auction, partners work together to decide how many tricks they believe they can win.

Once the auction ends, one partnership becomes the attacking side while the other partnership defends. Every hand becomes a fun puzzle involving teamwork, planning, and careful play.

Good News!

Bridge has a reputation for being difficult, but the basic rules are much easier than most people expect. You can learn enough to enjoy your first game in one afternoon.

The Basics

The Deck

Bridge uses a normal 52-card deck with no jokers. Every player receives exactly 13 cards.

Suit Ranking for Bidding

When bids are compared, suits have an order.

  1. ♣ Clubs
  2. ♦ Diamonds
  3. ♥ Hearts
  4. ♠ Spades
  5. No Trump (NT)

No Trump is not a suit. It means there is no trump suit.

Partnerships

North and South play together. East and West play together. Partners sit opposite one another.

The Deal

The dealer shuffles the cards. One card is dealt at a time clockwise until everyone has exactly 13 cards. No player should see another player's cards.

What Is A Trick?

A trick is made up of one card from each player. The first player leads a card. The other three players each play one card. The winning card takes the trick.

The Auction (Bidding)

The auction decides the contract. The contract tells everyone:

  • Which partnership will try to make the contract.
  • How many tricks they must win.
  • Which suit is trump—or whether there is No Trump.

How Bidding Works

  1. The dealer begins.
  2. Players take turns clockwise.
  3. You may bid, pass, double, or redouble.
  4. The auction ends after three passes in a row following a bid.

Pass

You choose not to make a bid.

Double

The opponents' contract becomes riskier and worth more points. Beginners don't need to worry about using doubles right away.

Redouble

A response to a double. This increases the stakes even more.

Legal Bids

Bids begin at 1♣ and continue upward through:

1♣ → 1♦ → 1♥ → 1♠ → 1NT then 2♣ ... all the way to 7NT.

1♥ Pass 2♥ Pass
4♥ Pass Pass Pass

Final Contract: 4 Hearts

What Does "4 Hearts" Mean?

A contract always promises six tricks plus the bid level.

  • 1-level = 7 tricks
  • 2-level = 8 tricks
  • 3-level = 9 tricks
  • 4-level = 10 tricks
  • 5-level = 11 tricks
  • 6-level = 12 tricks
  • 7-level = 13 tricks

So 4♥ means the declaring partnership must win at least 10 tricks with Hearts as trump.

Declarer

The first player from the partnership who bid the final trump suit (or No Trump) becomes the Declarer.

Dummy

The Declarer's partner becomes the Dummy. Their cards are placed face up on the table after the opening lead.

Playing the Hand

Opening Lead

The player to the Declarer's left makes the first lead. Only after this card is played does the Dummy place all 13 cards face up.

Following Suit

If someone leads a Heart and you have at least one Heart, you must play a Heart. You may choose any Heart from your hand.

Only when you have no Hearts may you play another suit or a trump.

Winning a Trick

If nobody plays a trump, the highest card of the suit that was led wins the trick. If one or more trump cards are played, the highest trump wins the trick.

Who Plays Dummy?

The Dummy does not choose cards. The Declarer decides which Dummy card to play on every trick.

Remember

  • Defenders try to stop the contract.
  • Declarer tries to make the contract.
  • Every trick matters.
  • Think before playing each card.
